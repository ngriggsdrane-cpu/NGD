The Campaign

In 2014, the same year she accepted an Academy Award, Lupita Nyong'o was diagnosed with uterine fibroids. Surgery removed 25 of them. A decade later, they returned — more than doubling. By 2026, Nyong'o carried 77 fibroids in total: 25 removed, over 50 still growing, the largest the size of an orange.

For years she said nothing. "It looked like shame," she told ABC News anchor Linsey Davis. "I was hiding in plain sight." But on her 43rd birthday, March 1, 2026, she chose visibility over silence. She posted a photograph of herself holding 77 pieces of fruit — blueberries, grapes, lemons, oranges, grapefruit, cantaloupe — each one representing a fibroid. The image became a symbol. Her caption read: "This is not a rare story, just a rarely told one."

The same week, she announced the Make Fibroids Count campaign with the Foundation for Women's Health, including the FWH × Lupita Nyong'o Uterine Fibroid Grant — a $200,000 initiative funding cutting-edge research into minimally invasive and non-invasive treatments for a condition that affects 26 million American women.

The numbers behind the silence are staggering: 80% of all women will develop fibroids in their lifetime. Women make up 51% of the population, yet receive only 8% of NIH research dollars — and just 0.03% of that goes to fibroid research. In March 2026, Nyong'o brought that data to Washington alongside fellow advocate Mandy Moore and Senator Angela Alsobrooks (D-MD), advocating for the bipartisan U-FIGHT Act, which would expand early screening, detection, and intervention access for patients diagnosed with uterine fibroids.
