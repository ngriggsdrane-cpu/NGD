The Campaign

Venus Williams has lived the data. As a Black woman who has publicly navigated her own health battles — including a fibroid diagnosis — she understands what it means when the medical system fails to listen, treats pain as inconsequential, or provides care unevenly across race and income.

In partnership with Saving Mothers, the nonprofit improving reproductive and maternal health worldwide, and NYU Langone, Williams has channeled her platform into direct advocacy for maternal health equity. The partnership spotlights the urgent reality: the United States maternal mortality rate has doubled in 20 years and is now higher than any other industrialized nation. At least half of those deaths are preventable.

Black pregnant women die at 2.6 times the rate of their white counterparts — a disparity driven by systemic racism, underinvestment in Black women's healthcare, and the persistent dismissal of women's pain by medical professionals. Williams brings her voice and her visibility to changing that narrative.

NYU Langone's Institute for Excellence in Health Equity received $12.5 million over five years to develop digital interventions addressing the maternal mortality crisis — work that runs parallel to the awareness and advocacy work Williams and Saving Mothers advance every day.
